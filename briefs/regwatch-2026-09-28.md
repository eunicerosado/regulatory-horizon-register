## Iran (Sanctions) (Amendment) Regulations 2026 take effect on 29 September, adding sectoral trade bans and financial, investment and brokering prohibitions that reach indirect and third-country activity

**FCDO · UK · legislation** · Iran (Sanctions) (Amendment) Regulations 2026

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-08 · effective 2026-09-29

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations

Amends the Iran (Sanctions) (Nuclear) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019 and the Iran (Sanctions) Regulations 2023. New trade prohibitions cover energy, precious metals, maritime technology, natural gas, oil products, petrochemicals and sectoral software. New financial restrictions cover loans, credit, investment, banking relationships and dealings in Iranian sovereign bonds, and the financial services and brokering prohibitions extend to indirect and third-country activity. Published by Notice to Exporters 2026/18. The indirect and third-country reach is the perimeter point: exposure is not limited to payments that touch Iran on their face, so trade finance and correspondent books need reviewing for the new sectors rather than only screening for names. Continues the thread at 2026-09-14-uk-001.

**Action:** Confirm screening, trade finance and correspondent banking controls reflect the new sectoral prohibitions and the financial restrictions from 29 September 2026, including brokering and indirect or third-country activity. Review existing loans, credit, investment and banking relationships and any holdings of or dealings in Iranian sovereign bonds.

## Council prolongs the Ukraine territorial-integrity listings for three years, to 22 September 2029, covering more than 3,000 persons and entities

**Council · EU · sanctions action** · Council Decision 2014/145/CFSP; Council Regulation (EU) No 269/2014

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-22 · expiry 2029-09-22

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, legal

The restrictive measures against those undermining Ukraine's territorial integrity, sovereignty and independence are prolonged for three years, with a new expiry of 22 September 2029, recorded in the deadline field as an expiry. They cover more than 3,000 individuals and entities and comprise travel restrictions, asset freezes and a prohibition on making funds or economic resources available. In the course of the renewal the Council removed three deceased persons and discontinued the listings of three individuals and one entity. The release does not state the previous renewal period, so the change to note is the three-year horizon itself: the Council's renewal date no longer prompts a periodic re-check of these screening records, and that refresh now depends on the firm's own review calendar. The delistings arrive as an amendment inside the renewal rather than as a new tranche.

**Action:** Confirm the delistings made in this renewal have been applied to screening lists and frozen-asset records. Check that internal periodic review of these listings does not rely on the Council's renewal date.

Source: <https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/09/22/ukraine-s-territorial-integrity-eu-extends-individual-listings-for-further-three-years/> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## FTC asks whether to update the Impersonation Rule to address platforms' role in promoting impersonation scams - comment deadline not yet confirmed

**FTC · US · consultation paper** · Rule on Impersonation of Government and Businesses

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-24

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, e money institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, compliance

Advance notice of proposed rulemaking asking whether to update the Rule on Impersonation of Government and Businesses, or take other action, to address how digital platforms' advertising optimisation practices help scammers impersonate legitimate businesses and government agencies. The FTC cites nearly $3.5 billion in reported impersonation losses in 2025, more than one million imposter scam reports, $2.1 billion in social-media-related losses and around 30% of victims first contacted on social media. DATE UNCONFIRMED - comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register, and that publication date has not been confirmed, so the deadline field is deliberately left empty and this item will not appear in the deadline calendar until it is set. Existing fraud liability regimes put the duty on the institution nearest the money; this asks whether the party that sold the reach should carry some of it, which is why it is recorded for banks and payment firms rather than platforms.

**Action:** Monitor for Federal Register publication and set the comment deadline when confirmed. Firms whose brand is impersonated in paid search and social ads should consider whether to put their own impersonation data on the record in a comment.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/ftc-seeks-public-comment-whether-update-rule-impersonation-government-businesses-address-platforms> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## ESMA sets a Union Strategic Supervisory Priority on digital innovation from 2027, covering how supervised firms deploy AI and tokenisation

**ESMA · EU · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-23

*Who it catches:* investment firms, fund managers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* risk, technology, compliance

A new Union Strategic Supervisory Priority on digital innovation, to run from 2027 - month not yet stated, so no date is recorded in the effective field - covering how supervised entities deploy artificial intelligence and tokenisation, delivered jointly with national competent authorities. ESMA's stated aim is to ensure supervisors have the expertise and capacity to oversee the use of new technologies. A Union Strategic Supervisory Priority means national supervisors ask the same questions in the same period, so the questions are being written now. Because the stated constraint is supervisory capability, the first examinations are likely to test what can be assessed in a meeting - documentation, explainability and human oversight - rather than model performance. Direction of travel, hence actionability 2.

**Action:** Inventory AI and tokenisation use cases in scope, including AI components in financial crime monitoring, screening and KYC, and confirm each has a documented purpose, a named owner and evidence of human oversight before 2027.

Source: <https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-sets-new-supervisory-priority-digital-innovation-2027> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## AMLA consults on draft RTS on cross-border information exchange between Financial Intelligence Units, closing 6 October

**AMLA · EU · consultation paper**

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-07-06 · consultation close 2026-10-06

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, technology

Consultation on draft Regulatory Technical Standards on cross-border information exchange between Financial Intelligence Units. Opened 6 July; closes 6 October 2026 at 23:59 CEST. Recorded this week, although published earlier, because the close falls inside the 60-day calendar; it was the only AMLA consultation still open as at the 28 September edition. The standards govern exchange between FIUs rather than reporting by firms, so the population with most at stake is firms filing into more than one national FIU, whose reports may travel between them.

**Action:** Respond by 6 October 2026, 23:59 CEST, if at all. MLROs and SAR teams filing into more than one FIU should read the draft for how their reports would be shared.

Source: <https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/public-consultations> · re-checked 2026-09-29
