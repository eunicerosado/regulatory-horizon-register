## Iran (Sanctions) (Amendment) Regulations 2026 take effect on 29 September, adding sectoral trade bans and financial, investment and brokering prohibitions that reach indirect and third-country activity

**FCDO · UK · legislation** · Iran (Sanctions) (Amendment) Regulations 2026

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-08 · effective 2026-09-29

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations

Amends the Iran (Sanctions) (Nuclear) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019 and the Iran (Sanctions) Regulations 2023. New trade prohibitions cover energy, precious metals, maritime technology, natural gas, oil products, petrochemicals and sectoral software. New financial restrictions cover loans, credit, investment, banking relationships and dealings in Iranian sovereign bonds, and the financial services and brokering prohibitions extend to indirect and third-country activity. Published by Notice to Exporters 2026/18. The indirect and third-country reach is the perimeter point: exposure is not limited to payments that touch Iran on their face, so trade finance and correspondent books need reviewing for the new sectors rather than only screening for names. Continues the thread at 2026-09-14-uk-001.

**Action:** Confirm screening, trade finance and correspondent banking controls reflect the new sectoral prohibitions and the financial restrictions from 29 September 2026, including brokering and indirect or third-country activity. Review existing loans, credit, investment and banking relationships and any holdings of or dealings in Iranian sovereign bonds.

Source: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/notice-to-exporters-202618-iran-sanctions-amendments-effective-from-29-september-2026/nte-202618-iran-sanctions-amendments-effective-from-29-september-2026> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## FCA survey of 35 firms records 238,396 suspected money mule accounts closed in 2025 and says firms need to do more on early detection

**FCA · UK · report**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.8) · actionability 3/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-23

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, e money institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, compliance

Survey of 35 financial institutions. Suspected mule accounts closed rose from 184,935 in 2023 to 233,269 in 2024 and 238,396 in 2025. Closures were highest among customers aged 26-39 at 91,073 and grew fastest in the 40-49 group, 37,274 in 2025 against 25,760 in 2024; customers aged 25 and under accounted for 85,425. The operative finding is not the volume but the statement that criminals move funds through multiple accounts before cashing out, typically between the second and fifth account. A detection programme concentrated on the first receiving account is working the layer the criminal expects to lose, while the accounts two to five hops out present as ordinary customers receiving ordinary sterling transfers from ordinary UK banks. That is a monitoring design question rather than a tuning one. Actionability 3 because no rule changed and no date attaches, but the FCA has stated an expectation firms must be able to evidence a position against. The FCA describes working with the National Crime Agency, the Home Office and HM Treasury on intelligence sharing, and a public-private working cell established with 22 regulated firms in 2025.

**Action:** Establish where in the chain your mule closures actually sit. If they cluster at the first receiving account, the closure count measures effort rather than effectiveness. Review whether monitoring can see an account whose inbound is almost entirely from other UK current accounts, held briefly and paid out fast, and review what is passed to the next firm at the point of closure.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/firms-crack-down-money-mules-need-do-more> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## Council prolongs the Ukraine territorial-integrity listings for three years, to 22 September 2029, covering more than 3,000 persons and entities

**Council · EU · sanctions action** · Council Decision 2014/145/CFSP; Council Regulation (EU) No 269/2014

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-22 · expiry 2029-09-22

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, legal

The restrictive measures against those undermining Ukraine's territorial integrity, sovereignty and independence are prolonged for three years, with a new expiry of 22 September 2029, recorded in the deadline field as an expiry. They cover more than 3,000 individuals and entities and comprise travel restrictions, asset freezes and a prohibition on making funds or economic resources available. In the course of the renewal the Council removed three deceased persons and discontinued the listings of three individuals and one entity. The release does not state the previous renewal period, so the change to note is the three-year horizon itself: the Council's renewal date no longer prompts a periodic re-check of these screening records, and that refresh now depends on the firm's own review calendar. The delistings arrive as an amendment inside the renewal rather than as a new tranche.

**Action:** Confirm the delistings made in this renewal have been applied to screening lists and frozen-asset records. Check that internal periodic review of these listings does not rely on the Council's renewal date.

Source: <https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/09/22/ukraine-s-territorial-integrity-eu-extends-individual-listings-for-further-three-years/> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## Twenty-one CFD firms closed since 2025 and three cancelling permissions, after the FCA found UK authorisation used as a badge for linked overseas companies

**FCA · UK · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.0) · actionability 3/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-09-25

*Who it catches:* investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, risk

Recorded as enforcement, though the published action is a mix of supervisory and enforcement measures rather than a single Final Notice: trading restrictions at some firms, required independent business reviews, and two enforcement investigations in the most serious cases. The FCA found firms doing minimal UK business that used their UK authorisation, in the FCA's words, as a badge to make linked overseas companies look more trustworthy than they really are, leaving consumers believing they held UK protections that did not apply. This is a perimeter case rather than a conduct case, and the structure it lands on is common well beyond CFDs: payments, crypto, wealth and brokerage groups routinely hold one UK permission while serving most clients through offshore affiliates. Reach 3 rather than 4 because the exposure follows a business model rather than a licence category. The consequence for third parties is that an FCA authorisation stops answering a diligence question and starts one: authorised for what, at what volume, with whom sharing the name.

**Action:** Groups holding a UK authorisation alongside non-UK affiliates that share a brand, website or client-acquisition funnel should be able to evidence how the authorisation is presented to consumers, and should expect group structure to be treated as a supervisory artefact. Firms providing accounts or services to such groups should stop treating authorised status as a diligence answer.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/twenty-four-cfd-firms-closing-crackdown-misuse-uk-authorisation> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## FTC asks whether to update the Impersonation Rule to address platforms' role in promoting impersonation scams - comment deadline not yet confirmed

**FTC · US · consultation paper** · Rule on Impersonation of Government and Businesses

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-24

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, e money institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, compliance

Advance notice of proposed rulemaking asking whether to update the Rule on Impersonation of Government and Businesses, or take other action, to address how digital platforms' advertising optimisation practices help scammers impersonate legitimate businesses and government agencies. The FTC cites nearly $3.5 billion in reported impersonation losses in 2025, more than one million imposter scam reports, $2.1 billion in social-media-related losses and around 30% of victims first contacted on social media. DATE UNCONFIRMED - comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register, and that publication date has not been confirmed, so the deadline field is deliberately left empty and this item will not appear in the deadline calendar until it is set. Existing fraud liability regimes put the duty on the institution nearest the money; this asks whether the party that sold the reach should carry some of it, which is why it is recorded for banks and payment firms rather than platforms.

**Action:** Monitor for Federal Register publication and set the comment deadline when confirmed. Firms whose brand is impersonated in paid search and social ads should consider whether to put their own impersonation data on the record in a comment.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/ftc-seeks-public-comment-whether-update-rule-impersonation-government-businesses-address-platforms> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## ESMA sets a Union Strategic Supervisory Priority on digital innovation from 2027, covering how supervised firms deploy AI and tokenisation

**ESMA · EU · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-23

*Who it catches:* investment firms, fund managers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* risk, technology, compliance

A new Union Strategic Supervisory Priority on digital innovation, to run from 2027 - month not yet stated, so no date is recorded in the effective field - covering how supervised entities deploy artificial intelligence and tokenisation, delivered jointly with national competent authorities. ESMA's stated aim is to ensure supervisors have the expertise and capacity to oversee the use of new technologies. A Union Strategic Supervisory Priority means national supervisors ask the same questions in the same period, so the questions are being written now. Because the stated constraint is supervisory capability, the first examinations are likely to test what can be assessed in a meeting - documentation, explainability and human oversight - rather than model performance. Direction of travel, hence actionability 2.

**Action:** Inventory AI and tokenisation use cases in scope, including AI components in financial crime monitoring, screening and KYC, and confirm each has a documented purpose, a named owner and evidence of human oversight before 2027.

Source: <https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-sets-new-supervisory-priority-digital-innovation-2027> · re-checked 2026-09-28

## EBA sets out four priorities for the Commission's MiCA review, led by multi-issuer stablecoin schemes involving third-country issuers

**EBA · EU · report**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-24

*Who it catches:* crypto asset service providers, credit institutions, e money institutions  
*Whose desk:* risk, compliance, legal, financial crime

Four priorities put to the European Commission ahead of its review of the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation: strengthening the framework for multi-issuer stablecoin schemes involving third-country issuers, which the EBA describes as carrying significant to very significant risks, together with a review of reserve requirements; clarifying scope and definitions, where classification difficulties create avoidable costs and delays in product roll-out; regulating crypto-asset lending, particularly where providers facilitate access to decentralised finance protocols; and strengthening the reporting framework for issuers and crypto-asset service providers. Actionability 2 because this is advice toward a future review rather than a change, but the planning assumptions move now. The multi-issuer point is the one with money attached: where the same token is issued inside and outside the EU, the EU-regulated leg carries the redemption promise while part of the reserve and the issuance discipline sit under another regime. The lending priority closes a perimeter argument several CASPs have built product on, namely that facilitating access to DeFi is non-custodial and therefore out of scope. The EBA frames none of this as AML, but classification determines which obliged-entity obligations attach, so the financial crime consequence arrives second.

**Action:** Issuers operating a scheme with a non-EU co-issuer, and CASPs offering lending, yield or routed access to DeFi, should establish now which leg of the structure would be answerable in the EU under each priority, rather than after the review reports.

Source: <https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-identifies-priorities-review-mica> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## SEC censures OTC Link LLC and imposes a $575,000 penalty for nine years of unremediated Regulation SCI policy failures identified across multiple examinations

**SEC · US · enforcement** · Regulation SCI Rules 1001(a)(1), 1001(a)(2), 1001(a)(3)

Materiality: **Low** (1.4) · actionability 1/5 · reach 2/5

Published 2026-09-22

*Who it catches:* investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, technology, risk

Censure and a $575,000 civil penalty for failing to establish, maintain and enforce written policies and procedures required by Regulation SCI between August 2016 and March 2025, covering system security, access control and application vulnerability management for the firm's alternative trading system. Scored 1 for actionability under the rubric because it is an enforcement outcome imposing no new obligation, and 2 for reach because SCI entities are a narrow population. Recorded for continuity rather than briefed, because the transferable point only becomes visible if a second action lands on the same root cause. That point is that the conduct priced here is not the control gap but the nine-year failure to close it after examiners identified it: the SEC said the repeated failure to address deficiencies showed a disregard for their findings and the overall examinations process. That converts a technology deficiency into a governance finding, and the read-across for any regulated firm is the age of the oldest item on its own remediation log.

**Action:** None arising directly. Firms outside the SCI population may wish to establish how many examination cycles their oldest open finding has survived, since that is the fact the SEC priced here.

Source: <https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-91-sec-censures-otc-link-llc-repeated-compliance-failures-related-regulation-sci> · re-checked 2026-09-29
