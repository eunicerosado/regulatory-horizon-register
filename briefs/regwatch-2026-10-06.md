## FCA opens the cryptoasset authorisation gateway, with applications from firms intending to continue UK operations due by 28 February 2027 and the regime commencing 25 October 2027

**FCA · UK · rule final** · FCA cryptoasset authorisation gateway

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-30 · effective 2027-10-25 · reporting date 2027-02-28

*Who it catches:* crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, legal, onboarding, board

The FCA began accepting authorisation applications from cryptoasset firms on 30 September 2026, the first formal authorisation route for UK crypto activity. Firms that intend to continue operating in the UK should apply by 28 February 2027. The regime commences on 25 October 2027 and the FCA expects to decide applications submitted in the window before then; firms that applied in time and have no decision by commencement may continue operating while under review. Firms that do not meet the standards will not be authorised and cannot continue offering regulated cryptoasset services in the UK. The dated deadline and the published prospect of refusal are what carry the score: this is a pre-commencement assessment rather than a registration, so the binding event for an applicant is the quality of the submission, not the filing date. Second-order effect for banks and EMIs serving the sector: a cohort of customers will have a known date on which UK permission ends, which is an exit-management question rather than an onboarding one. Confirms the 28 February 2027 date that 2026-09-23-uk-001 recorded as unconfirmed and kept out of its date fields, and re-confirms the 30 September 2026 opening and 25 October 2027 commencement stated there. Instrument type is recorded as rule_final because the dates attach to a regime already made in final form; the artefact itself is the FCA's announcement that the gateway is open, not a new rule, and the page frames the date as one by which firms intending to continue operating should apply. The deadline is recorded as reporting_date because it is a submission falling due; the instrument applies on 25 October 2027. Continues the thread at 2026-09-23-uk-001.

**Action:** Cryptoasset firms intending to continue UK operations: confirm the application is in train against the 28 February 2027 date, and that the business-wide risk assessment, the named MLRO's experience and authority, and transaction monitoring tuning and evidence would withstand supervisory reading. Firms banking or providing payment rails to the sector: record each customer's application status and plan for the 25 October 2027 commencement date.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-opens-gateway-regulated-crypto> · re-checked 2026-10-06

## OFSI opens the annual frozen asset review, with reports from all persons holding or controlling funds of a designated person due by 30 November 2026

**OFSI · UK · guidelines** · Annual frozen asset review and reporting form

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-30 · reporting date 2026-11-30

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations, finance

OFSI updated its annual frozen asset review guidance and reporting form on 30 September 2026. All persons that hold or control funds or economic resources belonging to a designated person must report, so that HM Treasury's records reflect changes over the reporting period. The deadline is Monday 30 November 2026. The reporting period itself is set out in the guidance notice rather than on the landing page and is not recorded here. The review is one of the few scheduled occasions on which a firm's sanctions data is tested for accuracy rather than coverage: current value, which designation each freeze rests on, and whether that designation is still live. That matters more than usual after a year of list churn — OFAC alone published a tranche of removals on 5 October 2026, largely IRAQ2 plus Colombian, Venezuelan and Belarusian entries.

**Action:** Identify all funds and economic resources held or controlled for designated persons, reconcile current values, confirm the designation each freeze rests on is still live, and submit the review by 30 November 2026. Treat discrepancies — frozen assets with no live designation behind them, and designated persons whose assets were never identified — as findings rather than form-filling.

## AMLA finalises three sets of RTS covering business relationships and occasional transactions, customer due diligence, and group-wide arrangements, proposed to apply six months after entry into force

**AMLA · EU · technical standard** · AMLA final draft RTS — business relationships and occasional transactions; customer due diligence; group-wide arrangements

Materiality: **High** (4.4) · actionability 4/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-10-01

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, onboarding, data, board

AMLA finalised three sets of regulatory technical standards on 1 October 2026 and submitted them to the European Commission. The first distinguishes business relationships from occasional transactions and defines when transactions are linked. The second covers customer due diligence: the information that must be collected, the methods for verifying it, and politically exposed person screening. The third covers group-wide arrangements — governance, risk management, internal controls and information sharing. Once the Commission adopts them and they are published in the Official Journal, AMLA proposes they apply six months after entry into force. Football agents and professional football clubs have an extended deadline of 10 July 2029. No adoption or Official Journal date has been published, so no effective date or deadline is recorded; actionability is scored 4 rather than 5 for that reason, not because the obligation is soft. The operative change is definitional: a harmonised test for business relationships and linked transactions reclassifies a population of customers that currently sits below the line and is already onboarded, already transacting, and held in files built for the old answer. Six months after entry into force is not a remediation window for a back book. The group-wide RTS makes a group policy that exists on paper but is not evidenced in subsidiaries a supervisory finding rather than an internal audit point.

**Action:** Identify where the firm currently draws the business-relationship versus occasional-transaction line and on what basis, and size the customer population that a harmonised linked-transaction test would move across it. Review the CDD information and verification standard and the PEP screening ruleset against the final draft RTS. For groups, confirm the group-wide policy is evidenced in each subsidiary rather than issued to it. Track Commission adoption to fix the application date.

Source: <https://www.amla.europa.eu/press-release-amla-finalises-key-standards-private-sector_en> · re-checked 2026-10-06

## FinCEN proposes a special measure prohibiting transmittals of funds involving six named A7 Network Sub-Agents, with comments closing 4 November 2026

**FinCEN · US · rule proposed** · Proposal of special measure prohibiting the transmittal of funds regarding transactions involving the A7 Network's Sub-Agents; 91 FR document 2026-20371

Materiality: **Medium** (3.8) · actionability 3/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-10-05 · consultation close 2026-11-04

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, money service businesses, payment service providers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, compliance, technology

FinCEN's proposed special measure was published in the Federal Register on 5 October 2026, acting under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act (Public Law 116-283) as amended by section 6106(b) of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022 (Public Law 117-81). It would prohibit covered financial institutions from transmitting funds to or from any A7 Network Sub-Agent, or from or to any account or convertible virtual currency address administered by or on behalf of one; require institutions to notify affected parties when a transmittal is declined; require risk-based screening to identify such transactions; and require documentation of compliance with the notification requirement. Six Sub-Agents are named: Galadriel Trading FZCO, Gimli Trade LLC-FZ, Hydrofusion Resources FZ-LLC, Pearl Bridge, Power Sphere LLC-FZ and Sigizmund FZCO. The measure is not in force, so actionability is scored 3 rather than 5 — firms are expected to be able to evidence a position, including on the notification duty, which most payment operations have never built as an evidenced workflow. Reach is scored 5 because covered financial institutions span banks, money services businesses and virtual asset firms rather than one licence category. Note the divergence worth publishing: the 6 October edition treated the A7 action as the most consequential item of the week on editorial judgment, while the rubric bands it Medium because the prohibition is proposed rather than binding.

**Action:** Screen for the six named Sub-Agents and any account or convertible virtual currency address administered on their behalf. Determine whether a declined-transmittal notification workflow exists and is evidenced, and whether it could be built to the proposed standard. Consider responding by 4 November 2026 on the operational cost of the notification and documentation requirements.

Source: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20371/proposal-of-special-measure-prohibiting-the-transmittal-of-funds-regarding-transactions-involving> · re-checked 2026-10-06

## FinCEN alert on the A7 Network sets document-based and infrastructure-based red flags and a SAR key term, alongside OFAC's designation of the network as a significant transnational criminal organisation

**FinCEN · US · advisory** · FIN-2026-Alert007

Materiality: **Medium** (3.8) · actionability 3/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-10-01

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, money service businesses, payment service providers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, technology, data

FinCEN issued Alert FIN-2026-Alert007 on 1 October 2026, urging institutions to detect, identify and report suspicious activity involving the A7 Network, which Treasury describes as a shadow banking network with ties to Russia used by the Iranian regime to evade sanctions. OFAC designated the network as a significant transnational criminal organisation. SARs should carry the key term "FIN-2026-A7NETWORK" in field 2 and in the narrative. The red flags fall into three groups: Sub-Agent usage, including shell companies in the Kyrgyz Republic, multi-jurisdictional payment routing, newly formed entities with unusual volumes and VPN access from the domains muzpan.com and sodkamus.com; invoice falsification, including anomalous shipping templates, implausible pricing, analog stamps on digital documents, Cyrillic characters in English-language documents and AI-generated modifications; and digital asset abuse, including A7A5 token exposure, stablecoin transfers to Sub-Agents and stablecoin use for commodity and military goods purchases. Named entities include A7 Limited Liability Company and subsidiaries A71 LLC and A7 Agent LLC, Promsvyazbank, Old Vector LLC, Garantex and Grinex. Jurisdictions named: Russia, Kyrgyz Republic, Iran, North Korea, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Seychelles, Türkiye and the United Arab Emirates. The structural point, and the reason this is recorded separately from the proposed rule: almost none of the red flags is name-based. They are document-based and infrastructure-based, and two of them — analog stamps on digital documents, AI-modified paperwork — are only visible to a person reading the document. Sanctions evasion has moved from entities to agency chains, and the control that detects an agency chain is competent document review.

**Action:** Circulate the alert's red flags to trade finance document review, payment operations and blockchain analytics, and confirm the three groups are reflected in monitoring and in reviewer guidance rather than only in name screening. Use the key term FIN-2026-A7NETWORK in field 2 and in the narrative of any related SAR. Check whether blockchain analytics and sanctions screening sit in separate queues with separate owners, which is where this typology falls between controls.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf> · re-checked 2026-10-06

## ESMA asks the Commission for powers to block fraudulent websites, freeze assets in suspected market abuse and regulate third-country firms soliciting EU investors in its MiCA review response

**ESMA · EU · report** · ESMA response to the European Commission consultation on the MiCA review

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-30

*Who it catches:* crypto asset service providers, credit institutions, payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, financial crime, risk

ESMA published its response to the Commission's MiCA review consultation on 30 September 2026. Alongside investor-protection asks — stricter marketing rules, cost transparency, and proportionate requirements with disclosure obligations for staking, lending and borrowing — it asked for a different class of tool: capacity to detect and block fraudulent websites, to freeze assets where abuse is suspected, and to regulate third-country firms soliciting EU investors. It also asked for clearer classification criteria for decentralised finance and hybrid tokens and for power to issue binding classification opinions, plus burden reduction on white papers and duplicate authorisations and a framework for tokenised securities and on-chain settlement. Nothing binds yet, which fixes actionability at 2. The reason to record it is that it is a different request from the EBA's MiCA review priorities recorded in the 28 September edition: that was about definitions, this is about powers. The third-country limb carries the commercial consequence — reverse solicitation is how a large share of offshore volume reaches EU customers, and ESMA is asking for that to be closed by rule rather than argued case by case. Binding classification opinions move AML scope decisions out of firms' hands, because classification determines which obliged-entity duties attach. Continues the thread at 2026-09-28-eu-004.

**Action:** No action required. Planning assumption to move: do not build a product or distribution model whose viability depends on reverse solicitation into the EU, or on a self-determined classification of a hybrid or DeFi token.

## SEC proposes a crypto asset custody framework for investment advisers and funds, permitting self-custody in certain conditions and allowing state trust companies as custodians

**SEC · US · rule proposed** · SEC Release 2026-100

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-10-01

*Who it catches:* fund managers, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, operations, risk, financial crime

The SEC proposed a framework on 1 October 2026 for how registered investment advisers and registered funds may custody crypto assets, amending rules under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and the Investment Company Act of 1940. It would permit self-custody in certain conditions and would allow state trust companies to act as custodians, and affects registered investment advisers, registered investment companies and business development companies. The comment period runs 60 days from Federal Register publication; the closing date was not stated in the release and no date is recorded here. The control consequence is the part the framing obscures: a qualified custodian is not only an asset-safety arrangement, it is an independent record of holdings and the thing an examiner reconciles against. Removing it turns a custody rule into a key-management, segregation-of-duties and attestation problem in a sector with thin control history in those areas. Where custody moves to a state trust company, the AML obligations attaching to the custodian depend on how that entity is characterised, against the long-unsettled history of the adviser AML programme rule in the US.

**Action:** No action required. Planning assumption to move: advisers considering self-custody should identify what replaces the custodian as the independent record of holdings, and who attests to it. Track Federal Register publication to fix the comment deadline.
