## OFAC designates a UK-proscribed protest group, an Italian hosting provider and an advocacy network under EO 13224, with a wind-down general licence

**OFAC · US · sanctions action**

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-26

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations, onboarding

Three organisations designated - Autistici Inventati (Italy), Palestine Action (UK) and Masar Badil - plus two individuals in Germany and Brazil. Counter Terrorism General License 36 authorises wind-down of transactions involving Autistici Inventati; Russia-related GL 104B was amended the same day. The list ingestion is routine. The exposure is not: none of these is a financial institution, so matches surface where screening and monitoring are least tuned - donation and grant flows, card and subscription payments, hosting and domain fees, small supplier invoices. Most transaction monitoring de-prioritises all of those by value. The cross-border limb is separate and is the one that catches UK and EU firms: EO 13224 bites on US nexus, so a firm clearing USD now carries a US obligation over a UK-based entity while its domestic listing position remains a different question with a different answer. A wind-down licence exists because counterparties have live contracts.

**Action:** Run this week's additions against supplier, payee and grant files, not only the customer file. Confirm whether monitoring would raise a recurring two- or three-figure payment to a newly designated entity at all. Where the firm has US nexus and a UK or EU listing position that differs, establish which obligation the payment operation actually applies, and record the reasoning.

## Eight Venezuela-related general licences amended in one day, the third round of Venezuela general licence churn in three weeks

**OFAC · US · sanctions action**

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-27

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations, legal

GL 46D, 47B, 48C, 50C, 51C, 52B, 54B and 61A all reissued on 27 August, covering oil and petrochemicals, diluents, oil and gas sector operations, minerals including gold, PdVSA, and telecommunications. Two new FAQs, two amended, one archived. No expiry dates on the recent-actions page. This is the same failure mode recorded at 2026-08-23-us-002, now on its third round: an amended general licence keeps its number and changes its text, while internal permissions are almost always recorded as "permitted under GL 46" with the letter dropped. Any Venezuela-related approval granted before 27 August was granted against superseded conditions. The control that would catch this - a register tying each live relationship to a specific licence version so an amendment triggers re-check rather than a filing - is dull, rarely built, and invisible to every screening test.

**Action:** Re-read the text of each Venezuela general licence currently relied on rather than confirming the number is still current, and re-approve against the 27 August version. Identify who owns general licence version tracking and whether it lives in a controlled system or a spreadsheet. Treat this as the third occurrence, not an isolated one.

## FinCEN proposes a section 311 rule severing Banque Misr UAE from US correspondent banking, reaching into nested correspondent chains

**FinCEN · US · rule proposed**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-28

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations, risk

A finding that Banque Misr in the UAE is a financial institution of primary money laundering concern, proposed under section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act and placed within Operation Economic Outcast. It would prohibit US institutions from opening or maintaining a correspondent account for the bank and require reasonable steps not to process transactions involving it through any foreign bank's US correspondent account. That second limb is the one programmes handle badly: it reaches past the firm's own relationships into a correspondent's book, which is a payment-filtering and due-diligence question rather than a name-screening one. There is no SDN entry to ingest and no vendor feed refresh that resolves it. Note the scope is deliberately narrow - the finding is specific to Banque Misr's UAE operations, not the group - so a blunt block on the name over-restricts a bank the rule does not cover, and over-blocking carries its own supervisory conversation. Scored as a proposal, not a binding obligation; on the published rubric that caps actionability at 3 even though the practical exposure is the largest US item this week. If the rule is finalised in current terms, rescore.

**Action:** Read the notice text directly to establish the comment period and closing date - neither is stated on FinCEN's announcement page and the notice was not in the Federal Register FinCEN listing when checked on 30 August. Identify which correspondents plausibly serve UAE banking relationships; the search is for the corridor, not the name. Confirm any control built now distinguishes Banque Misr UAE from the wider group.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-proposes-rule-would-revoke-banque-misr-uaes-correspondent-banking-access> · re-checked 2026-08-30

## SEC charges 38 entities that filed false Forms ADV to appear on the adviser register as legitimate US firms

**SEC · US · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-27

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* onboarding, financial crime, compliance

Charges under sections 204(a) and 207 of the Investment Advisers Act over Forms ADV filed between 2025 and 2026. The described pattern is bogus Colorado addresses with no presence, disconnected or unrelated telephone numbers, near-identical ownership structures repeated across supposedly unrelated exempt reporting advisers, claimed auditors that cannot be found in any public registry, and fake SEC registration certificates on websites. The read-across is not about advisers, it is about registers: a filing register records what was submitted, not what is true, and the exempt reporting adviser route is a notice rather than an authorisation. "Verified on the regulator's register" appears in an enormous number of onboarding files and was demonstrated here to be a check on paperwork rather than on existence. Three of the four checks that would have caught this take a junior analyst ten minutes.

**Action:** Where a counterparty was accepted on the strength of a regulator register entry alone, add existence checks: does the address resolve, does the telephone number connect, does the named auditor appear in any registry. Test whether the same ownership structure repeats across nominally unrelated counterparties on the book. Decide whether a register screenshot remains sufficient evidence in the file.

Source: <https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-78-sec-38-entities-feigned-legitimacy-us-advisers-through-false-filings-lure-retail-investors> · re-checked 2026-08-30

## EBA consults on draft technical standards prescribing what an operational risk management framework must contain, with a EUR 750m proportionality threshold

**EBA · EU · consultation paper**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-08-26 · consultation close 2026-12-31

*Who it catches:* credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* risk, compliance, data, board

Draft RTS under Article 323(2) CRR as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1623, covering governance arrangements, the operational risk management process and the operational risk assessment system - including the roles of the management body, senior management and the independent operational risk management function, and requirements for data, taxonomy, business indicators, reporting, validation and audit. Institutions with a business indicator below EUR 750m get lower-frequency reviews, less granular data and simpler loss thresholds. Operational risk has been governed by principles, so every bank built a different framework and none were comparable; prescribed taxonomy and data requirements make a framework something a supervisor can hold next to a peer's. The proportionality threshold is where the planning sits - an institution near the line needs to know which side it will be on before the standards land. Note the overlap nobody owns: operational risk taxonomy, DORA incident classification and financial crime event data describe the same failures in three vocabularies, maintained in most banks by three teams.

**Action:** Establish which side of the EUR 750m business indicator threshold the institution sits on and how stable that position is. Compare the existing operational risk taxonomy against the draft categories and identify what would have to be recategorised. Two dates fall before the consultation closes and are not carried in this record's deadline field - registration for the EBA public hearing closes 25 September 2026 at 16:00 CEST, and the hearing runs 29 September 2026, 10:00-12:00 CEST.

Source: <https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-consults-draft-technical-standards-institutions-operational-risk-management> · re-checked 2026-08-30

## FCA bans three and fines two over a Dolfin-linked scheme that produced 99 UK investor visas at a fifth of the required investment

**FCA · UK · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-26

*Who it catches:* investment firms, fund managers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, onboarding, board

Conduct between 2016 and 2019 at Dolfin Financial (UK) Limited: clients paid around GBP 400,000 rather than making the GBP 2m investment the Home Office investor visa required, and at least 99 individuals obtained visas. The FCA found the scheme deliberately designed to create a false impression that requirements had been met, generating at least GBP 35.5m in fees. Denisz Nagy fined GBP 324,800 and banned; Sanjay Maraj fined GBP 122,000 and banned; Roman Joukovski banned as an undisclosed shadow director and controller without approval, and has referred to the Upper Tribunal. The transferable failing is the introducer channel, not the visa fraud: a migration agent paid per approved application has a direct financial interest in the file passing, and that conflict is usually recorded as a commercial marketing arrangement rather than a control risk. The shadow director finding is a governance failure no transaction monitoring would surface. Conduct ending 2019, outcomes in 2026, one case still live - a legacy book carries its history.

**Action:** Identify introducer and referral arrangements paid per completed application or per onboarded client, and assess them as conflicts rather than as commercial agreements. Confirm that everyone exercising control or direction over the firm holds the approval their conduct requires, not only those on the org chart.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-bans-trio-bypass-visa-rules> · re-checked 2026-08-30

## FCA research finds 44% of younger investors believe AI-generated financial information is regulated and 32% expect compensation if it goes wrong

**FCA · UK · report**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-27

*Who it catches:* investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, risk, board

Survey of 666 UK respondents aged 18-40 who own or are considering investments, fieldwork 24 July 2026. 80% of less experienced investors have used AI for investment help; 56% trust AI tools, ahead of TV and radio at 47%, press at 46% and influencers at 29%; 67% expect to rely on it more over the next year. The number with a P&L attached is the 32% who expect FSCS or Financial Ombudsman compensation if AI-based advice goes wrong, supported by the 44% who believe the information is regulated at all. It is not, and it will not be compensated - but the belief converts into a complaint, and the complaint arrives at the nearest regulated entity, which is the platform the trade executed on rather than the tool that suggested it. That is a redress and complaints-handling exposure created entirely outside the perimeter. Consumer understanding under the Consumer Duty is the obvious hook; the sharper question is distribution - what suitability and appropriateness testing actually catches when a customer arrives having already decided.

**Action:** Review complaints-handling and root-cause analysis for cases where the customer relied on an external AI tool, and decide now how they are categorised. Test whether consumer understanding material addresses what is and is not regulated and compensated. Distinguish this from existing governance over models the firm itself deploys - it is a different exposure and is not covered by that work.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/young-investors-trust-ai-more-tv-or-celebrities> · re-checked 2026-08-30
