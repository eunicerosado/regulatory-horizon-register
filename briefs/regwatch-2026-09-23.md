## Defence Against Money Laundering SAR threshold raised to £3,000, removing a tranche of low-value consent requests and leaving the judgment with the firm

**FCA · UK · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **High** (4.4) · actionability 4/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-17

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations

Stated in a speech by the FCA's Executive Director of Enforcement and Market Oversight at the Law Society Economic Crime Conference. The speech records the threshold as raised to £3,000, producing fewer low-value reports and more capacity to focus on higher-risk activity. Recorded as a supervisory statement because the change was announced in a speech rather than in a published instrument; no commencement date is given and none is recorded here. The consequence for firms is not volume relief but the transfer of a judgment. Below the threshold the consent request is no longer available as a statutory defence, so the transaction still needs a decision, the suspicion still needs handling, and the firm owns the outcome. Fewer reports means each remaining decision carries more weight.

**Action:** Re-paper the internal escalation standard for suspicions falling below the threshold, so that the judgment the consent request used to substitute for is documented and owned. Confirm the commencement of the change with the relevant published source before relying on the threshold operationally.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/financial-crime-protecting-hive> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## EBA final Guidelines on the management of third-party risk bring non-ICT providers into the same framework as ICT ones, with a two-year transitional period

**EBA · EU · guidelines**

Materiality: **High** (4.4) · actionability 4/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-18

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, investment firms, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* risk, operations, compliance, legal

Final Guidelines covering the full lifecycle of a third-party arrangement: risk assessment and due diligence, contracting, subcontracting, monitoring, documentation and exit strategies. They promote a holistic approach across ICT and non-ICT services and are scoped to arrangements supporting critical or important functions, where disruption would materially impair the performance of the financial entity. DORA already covers ICT third parties; the significant part here is that non-ICT providers come into the same framework, which for a financial crime function means the outsourced KYC bureau, the offshore alert-review team and the CDD remediation vendor move from a procurement folder into a resilience register. The proportionality cuts both ways: scoping by critical-or-important function means the first supervisory question becomes why a given arrangement is not critical. DATE UNCONFIRMED - the press release states a two-year transitional period but does not give a date of application, so no date is recorded here and none should be inferred from the transitional period alone.

**Action:** Identify third-party arrangements supporting critical or important functions, including non-ICT arrangements in the financial crime chain, and be able to evidence the basis on which arrangements were not so designated. Confirm the date of application from the Guidelines themselves when published in final form.

## FCA cryptoasset authorisation gateway opens on 30 September 2026, with the regime coming into force on 25 October 2027 and existing registrations not converting automatically

**FCA · UK · rule final** · PS26/18 Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance

Materiality: **High** (4.0) · actionability 4/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-16 · effective 2027-10-25

*Who it catches:* crypto asset service providers, e money institutions, payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, financial crime

Final perimeter guidance on which cryptoasset activities require authorisation. The press release states that the authorisation gateway for firms will open on 30 September 2026 and that the regime comes into force on 25 October 2027. The load-bearing point for firms already registered under the Money Laundering Regulations is that an MLR registration is a financial crime supervision status while the new permissions are conduct authorisations, with threshold conditions, a business model to defend and a senior management regime attached. Firms already authorised as e-money issuers or payment institutions may need a variation of permission rather than a fresh application, which changes the timetable. DATE UNCONFIRMED - the 23 September edition also recorded 28 February 2027 as a transitional-route application deadline, sourced to PS26/18. That date does not appear in this press release and was not re-verified on 29 September 2026, so it is deliberately not recorded in any date field here. It should be confirmed against PS26/18 itself before being relied on, because if correct it is the operative deadline rather than October 2027.

**Action:** Map current activities to the five authorisation buckets and determine whether a new authorisation or a variation of permission is required. Confirm the transitional-route deadline against PS26/18 directly and put that date, not the October 2027 commencement, in front of the board.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/crypto-firms-get-guidance-how-new-regime-applies> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## FCA to take on AML supervision of 60,000 legal and accounting sector entities from the back end of 2028

**FCA · UK · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-17

*Who it catches:* designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, legal

The same speech states that the FCA will begin to take on responsibility for AML supervision of 60,000 entities in the legal and accounting sectors at the back end of 2028, using a risk-based, intelligence-led approach. No month is stated, so no date is recorded in the effective field. This consolidates professional-body supervision into a statutory regulator with enforcement powers and a data operation, which addresses the most criticised gap in the UK AML regime. Direction of travel for now, hence actionability 2, but it changes who correspondent and client-account relationships with law firms and accountancy practices ultimately answer to, which is a planning assumption worth moving early.

**Action:** Firms banking or servicing legal and accountancy practices should note the supervisory transfer in their sector risk assessment and watch for the implementing instrument, which had not been published as at 29 September 2026.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/financial-crime-protecting-hive> · re-checked 2026-09-29

## AMLA consultation on draft RTS on assessing the inherent and residual risk profile of obliged entities in the non-financial sector, closed 27 September

**AMLA · EU · consultation paper**

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-07-13 · consultation close 2026-09-27

*Who it catches:* designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, risk

Consultation on draft Regulatory Technical Standards governing how supervisors assess the inherent and residual risk profile of obliged entities in the non-financial sector. Opened 13 July 2026 and closed 27 September 2026 at 23:59 CEST, both re-read on the AMLA consultations page on 29 September 2026. Recorded as closed. It is kept in the register rather than dropped because the resulting standards determine how non-financial obliged entities will be risk-rated by their supervisors, and the draft is the best available indication of the methodology ahead of the final text.

**Action:** The window to respond has passed. Non-financial obliged entities and their advisers should read the draft as the working indication of supervisory risk-rating methodology and watch for the final RTS.
