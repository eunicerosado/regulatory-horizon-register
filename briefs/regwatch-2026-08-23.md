## On 18 to 21 August 2026, OFAC issued three action sets

**OFAC · US · sanctions action** · OFAC recent actions 20260818, 20260820 and 20260821

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-18

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, technology

Three OFAC action sets on 18, 20 and 21 August, read on the dated recent-actions pages on 2026-11-19. On 18 August: International Criminal Court-related designations of two individuals, a Venezuela-related designation of a British Virgin Islands company, and ICC-related General License 12 authorising wind-down. On 20 August: counter-narcotics, counter-terrorism, Cuba-related and Iran-related designations, including an Ecuador-based cocaine trafficking network designated under Executive Order 14059 with several members also designated under Executive Order 13224, a Hizballah and IRGC-Qods Force cash network, and Cuban state-owned enterprises, together with Russia-related General License 131I and amended FAQs 1224, 1225 and 1265. On 21 August: no designations, but new Venezuela General Licenses 61 and 62 on telecommunications and FAQ 1266. The designations are routine list maintenance. The licence churn is the control point: Russia-related General License 131I is a lettered revision of an existing licence, so any control keyed to the licence number rather than its current text will silently apply superseded terms. Screening list ingestion is usually automated; general licence tracking usually is not. Verification on 2026-11-19 corrected two claims: designations were made on 18 and 20 August only, not on 21 August; and the Venezuela licences of 21 August were new issuances, so the earlier statement that Venezuela licences moved twice inside a fortnight is not supported by these pages. The source_url is OFAC's undated listing, which no longer shows August; the dated pages are cited in reference.

**Action:** Re-check the text of any Venezuela- and Russia-related general licence relied on, rather than confirming the GL number is still current. Confirm whether general-licence terms are tracked in a controlled system or a spreadsheet, and who owns expiry.

## FCA warns on unregulated mini-bonds and loan notes, and states an expectation that banks, PSPs and professional firms report suspicious distribution

**FCA · UK · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-20

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, onboarding

A consumer warning on unregulated loan notes and mini-bonds, using the failure of litigation funder Woodville Consultants Ltd as the case study; the FCA states it has issued over 1,200 warnings in 2026. Marketing speculative illiquid securities to retail investors has been banned since 1 January 2021. The industry-facing part is the significant one: the FCA cannot supervise an issuer outside the perimeter, so it supervises the firms that touch the money instead, and names banks, payment providers, lawyers, accountants and auditors as expected to report suspicious distribution. Affected customers have no FOS or FSCS recourse, which concentrates recovery pressure on whoever remains solvent and regulated.

**Action:** Confirm whether the FCA warning list is an input to adverse-media and customer screening. Be able to evidence what the firm did on becoming aware of an in-scope issuer among its customers or counterparties.

## On 17 August 2026, Doxo agreed to pay $2.1m to the FTC

**FTC · US · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-17

*Who it catches:* payment service providers, money service businesses  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, operations

In a 2024 complaint the FTC alleged that Doxo and co-founders Steve Shivers and Roger Parks used search ads and landing pages carrying billers' names, and sometimes their logos, to present Doxo as the official payment channel when it had no relationship with the overwhelming majority of those billers; added delivery fees that were not clearly disclosed; and deceptively signed consumers up to a recurring subscription. At the FTC's request a federal court found that Doxo violated the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act by failing to clearly disclose subscription terms and failing to obtain consent for subscription charges. Under the proposed stipulated order, approved by the Commission 2 to 0 and filed in the Western District of Washington, Doxo pays $2.1m for consumer redress, and the defendants are barred from misrepresenting affiliation with billers, using a biller's website address in any search advertisement, misrepresenting fees, and charging consumers without express informed consent. The order has the force of law only once the court approves and signs it. What makes this a control question rather than a news item is that the impersonation came from a company presenting itself as a legitimate payment channel, not from an external fraudster, which is precisely what impersonation detection is not built to catch. Verification on 2026-11-19 removed two claims the page does not support: that the court's finding came on partial summary judgment, and that Doxo had a compliance function.

**Action:** Establish whether the firm's paid-search keyword set and ad copy have ever been reviewed by second line, and who owns that review. Confirm whether brand-term bidding by third parties is monitored by the fraud function.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/bill-payment-firm-doxo-pay-21-million-settle-ftc-allegations-it-deceived-consumers-charged-them-add> · re-checked 2026-11-19

## On 19 August 2026, the FCA fined and banned the SVS ex-CEO

**FCA · UK · enforcement** · Final Notice 2026, Demetrios Christos Hadjigeorgiou

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-19

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, board

The FCA prohibited Demetrios Hadjigeorgiou from senior management functions and fined him £56,400 under sections 66 and 56 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, after he settled and withdrew his referral to the Upper Tribunal. While he was chief executive of SVS Securities, a discretionary fund manager, from 1 May 2018 to 7 August 2019, the firm invested customers' money, including pension savings, in high-risk products while receiving significant payments from their issuers, and he failed to challenge a 10% reduction applied to customers' bond investments on sale, which generated £359,800 for SVS. The FCA found a breach of Statement of Principle 6. The operative detail is not the fine. The Final Notice, read on 2026-11-19, records that the Decision Notice of 25 April 2024 imposed £84,600, and that on further consideration the FCA categorised the markdown conduct as a breach of Statement of Principle 6 rather than Statement of Principle 1, with no change to the factual findings, which produced the lower penalty; no settlement discount applied. The same facts, differently characterised, produce a materially different sanction, and that characterisation boundary is where SMCR defence arguments actually live.

**Action:** Monitor only. No obligation change. Worth reading the conduct sections of the Final Notice against current senior-manager fitness and propriety files, and noting the April 2024 to August 2026 interval when modelling enforcement timelines.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-bans-former-svs-securities-ceo> · re-checked 2026-11-19

## On 18 August 2026, the FCA banned Beauforce's Howard Duckett

**FCA · UK · enforcement**

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-08-18

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, board

The FCA banned Howard Roland Duckett, a senior manager at debt management firm Beauforce Corporation Limited approved for the SMF3 and SMF16 functions, from working in financial services for a serious lack of honesty and integrity. On 13 November 2020 the High Court disqualified him as a company director for 10 years, effective 4 December 2020, over records at an unrelated company, and found that he repeatedly lied and relied on fabricated evidence to deny he was a director, including falsely claiming that a fictitious individual ran the business. He did not tell the FCA about the disqualification. His reference of the FCA's Decision Notice to the Upper Tribunal was struck out, and the FCA had restricted Beauforce from regulated activities in November 2025. Read alongside the SVS notice published the following day, the pairing shows the FCA pursuing individuals rather than firms in the same week. The disqualification non-disclosure is the transferable point: it is a public record a firm can check for itself rather than relying on the individual to disclose it. Verification on 2026-11-19 corrected the record: the lying and fabricated evidence were findings of the High Court in the disqualification proceedings, not conduct towards the FCA as the record previously stated.

**Action:** Monitor only. Confirm that director disqualification registers are checked as part of senior-manager onboarding and periodic fitness and propriety review.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-bans-senior-manager-lack-honesty-and-integrity> · re-checked 2026-11-19

## ESMA consults on the reporting framework for clearing activity at recognised third-country CCPs

**ESMA · EU · consultation paper**

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-08-18 · consultation close 2026-10-12

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, operations, data

A consultation on how clearing activity at recognised third-country CCPs is to be reported. Low current materiality and high forward significance — the two are not the same thing. Reporting frameworks are how a supervisor assembles an evidence base before acting, and the question this one is built to answer is how much EU clearing still sits outside the EU. The obligation arriving now is data collection. Any relocation pressure would arrive later, on the strength of what the data shows.

**Action:** Consider responding. Feedback on the reporting framework, templates and format is invited by 12 October 2026, confirmed on the consultation page.
