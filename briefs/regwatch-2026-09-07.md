## FinCEN alert puts $12.7bn of suspicious activity on Southeast Asian scam centres and introduces the SAR key term FIN-2026-SCAMCENTERS

**FinCEN · US · advisory** · FIN-2026-Alert005

Materiality: **Medium** (3.8) · actionability 3/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-03

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, money service businesses, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, data

Alert and supporting financial trend analysis on laundering tied to digital asset investment scam centres run largely from Southeast Asia: approximately $12.7bn in suspicious activity across 33,904 BSA reports filed between 8 September 2023 and 31 December 2025, with victims in all 50 states and US territories. Three red-flag sets - victim payments, guarantee marketplaces, and laundering techniques. Institutions are asked to reference FIN-2026-SCAMCENTERS in SAR field 2 and the narrative, and to select Fraud-Other in field 34(z) with the description "Scam Centers." The filing mechanics are the easy part. Guarantee marketplaces - escrow services through which scam operators buy laundering, data and infrastructure from each other - describe a supply chain rather than a typology, so the entity monitoring flags is often several steps from the fraud. Six days later OFAC designated the largest of them, Xinbi Guarantee (2026-09-14-us-003). Once a key term exists, the volume and quality of filings against it becomes something a supervisor can measure.

**Action:** Add the key term and field 34(z) instruction to SAR procedures and case management. Map the three red-flag sets to existing scenarios and record where none applies. Confirm fraud and AML teams reconcile scam cases, so that a quarter with no filings under the key term reflects no exposure rather than two teams not talking.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-identifies-nearly-13-billion-linked-suspected-digital-asset-scams> · re-checked 2026-09-07

## FinCEN reissues the southwest border geographic targeting order, cutting the MSB cash reporting threshold to $1,000 in eight counties

**FinCEN · US · rule final** · Geographic Targeting Order, Federal Register doc. 2026-18194

Materiality: **Medium** (3.8) · actionability 5/5 · reach 2/5

Published 2026-09-02 · effective 2026-09-04 · expiry 2027-03-03

*Who it catches:* money service businesses, credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, operations, onboarding

A geographic targeting order rather than a rule - recorded as rule_final because it is a binding obligation with dates, which is what the label needs to convey here. Certain MSBs must file Currency Transaction Reports for cash transactions between $1,000 and $10,000 in specified ZIP codes of Bernalillo, Dona Ana and San Juan counties, New Mexico, and Cameron, El Paso, Hidalgo, Maverick and Webb counties, Texas. It runs for 180 days after Federal Register publication on 4 September 2026; the expiry recorded here is computed from that, not stated by FinCEN. The load falls hardest on the smallest firms - agents filing at a tenth of the usual threshold, often manually. The read-across for banks is the useful part: an MSB customer operating in these counties whose CTR volume does not rise this quarter is either not complying or not disclosing where it operates, which makes the order a free control test.

**Action:** MSBs in the named ZIP codes: confirm reporting at the $1,000 threshold is live, including at agent locations. Banks with MSB customers: identify which operate in the covered counties and add an expected rise in CTR volume to this quarter's reviews.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-reissues-order-requiring-transparency-msbs-along-southwest-border> · re-checked 2026-09-07

## FTC settlement makes Nuvei pay $4.85m and names load balancing across merchant accounts as evasion of fraud monitoring

**FTC · US · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-04

*Who it catches:* payment service providers, credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, onboarding, risk

Settlement with Nuvei Corporation and four affiliates on a 2-0 Commission vote. The FTC alleged Nuvei knowingly opened and kept processing accounts for deceptive merchants, including over $30m processed between 2017 and 2023 for Reimage, an offshore tech-support scam, charged under the FTC Act and the Telemarketing Sales Rule. Nuvei pays $4.85m and is banned from processing tech-support sales made by telemarketing or pop-up; must run enhanced screening of existing clients whose chargeback rates exceed set limits and monitor high-risk categories such as outbound telemarketing; and must not use tactics to evade bank or card-network fraud monitoring, "including load balancing." Naming load balancing reframes a long-open commercial practice as evasion of a control. If chargeback ratios are managed rather than measured, every downstream control that consumes them - merchant risk scoring, review triggers, sponsor bank oversight - runs on a shaped number. Four days later the FTC moved against Humboldt Merchant Services on the same pattern (2026-09-14-us-004); two actions in one week is a direction, not an incident.

**Action:** Establish whether any merchant's volume is split across multiple MIDs and whether chargeback ratios can be viewed across a single beneficial owner. Confirm existing merchants are re-screened, not only new ones, and whether high-risk merchant approval sits with someone carrying a revenue target.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/payment-processor-nuvei-must-implement-robust-merchant-screening-practices-pay-485-million-settle> · re-checked 2026-09-07

## OFSI imposes a £4.73m penalty on Citibank's London branch for making funds available for the benefit of a designated person, under two regimes

**OFSI · UK · enforcement** · s.146 Policing and Crime Act 2017

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-02

*Who it catches:* credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, technology

Penalty of £4,732,830.58 imposed on 11 August 2026 and published 2 September. OFSI's enforcement collection records the breach as "making funds available for the benefit of a designated person" under the Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019 and the Global Anti-Corruption Sanctions Regulations 2021. The penalty notice itself could not be read when this record was compiled, so transaction values, conduct dates, voluntary disclosure and any discount are not asserted here. Against OFSI's other published 2026 penalties - £1,000,920.59, £390,000, £165,000 and £160,000 - this is a step change. Two details carry the lesson. "For the benefit of" is a wider hook than "to", reaching indirect benefit through structures, which is closer to ownership and control analysis than to name screening. And pairing Russia with Global Anti-Corruption in one action suggests the thematic regimes that were not resourced after 2022 are where findings now come from.

**Action:** Read the penalty notice before briefing a board; this record is built from the publication and collection pages only. Test whether screening can detect indirect benefit to a designated person, not only a name in a payment field. Check the resourcing and tuning given to the smaller thematic regimes against the Russia programme.

Source: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/imposition-of-monetary-penalty-citibank-na-london-branch> · re-checked 2026-09-07

## FinCEN and the federal banking agencies state that SAR confidentiality does not stop banks warning customers about suspected fraud or explaining account closures

**FinCEN · US · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-02

*Who it catches:* credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* compliance, operations, financial crime

Joint statement by FinCEN, the FRB, FDIC, NCUA and OCC that SAR confidentiality requirements "do not preclude banks from communicating with their customers regarding potentially fraudulent transactions and other suspicious activity or account closures." The agencies state it "does not alter existing Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) legal or regulatory requirements or establish new supervisory expectations" - hence actionability 2. Nothing changed legally, and that is the point: "we can't tell you, it's a SAR" has served as a catch-all script in contact centres and closure letters, and regulators have now removed that cover in writing, turning an ambiguity into a training and conduct question. The second-order effect is on scam victims - a bank that could have said "this transfer looks like a scam" and said nothing has a weaker position with a supervisor and in any redress forum. Expect the statement to be cited back at firms more often than by them.

**Action:** Review fraud call scripts and account-closure templates for refusals justified on confidentiality grounds, and test each against what the rule actually protects. Confirm who owns those scripts, since the failure will occur in customer-facing teams rather than in financial crime.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-agencies-issue-joint-statement-suspicious-activity-report-confidentiality> · re-checked 2026-09-07

## EU customs reform adopted - the sub-EUR 150 duty exemption goes, non-EU e-commerce platforms become the importer, and a single customs data hub is created

**Council · EU · legislation**

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-03 · application date 2026-11-01

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, risk

Final Council approval of the customs overhaul. It creates an EU Customs Authority in Lille, operating from 2027, and an EU Customs Data Hub that will risk-score consignments. The duty exemption for imports under EUR 150 is removed and an EU-wide handling fee on small parcels is to be introduced by 1 November 2026 - the deadline field records that date, which the Council gives as "by". Non-EU e-commerce platforms are treated as the importer and made responsible for customs compliance, with penalties of up to 6% of annual import value. Hub use becomes mandatory for e-commerce businesses from 1 July 2028 and for all traders from 1 March 2034. The direct obligations fall on platforms and importers, a population outside the register's usual perimeter; the entity scope records the financial firms that feel it. For them this is a financial crime item: undervaluation of low-value consignments has been a durable trade-based laundering and duty-fraud technique precisely because sub-EUR 150 parcels attracted no duty and little scrutiny. The hub will eventually give trade-based money laundering controls a dataset they have always treated as unobtainable, and a penalty expressed as a percentage of import value changes the risk profile of marketplace clients.

**Action:** Identify marketplace, platform and cross-border e-commerce clients that will become the importer of record, and reassess their exposure given penalties scaled to import value. Note the hub dates in trade-based money laundering planning.

Source: <https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/09/03/eu-customs-council-greenlights-landmark-reform/> · re-checked 2026-09-07
