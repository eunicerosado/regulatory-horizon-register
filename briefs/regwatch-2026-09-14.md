## OFAC moves Iran licensing to a presumption of denial, prices individual liability at $1.4m, and FinCEN opens a paid Iran whistleblower channel, all inside three days

**OFAC · US · sanctions action**

Materiality: **High** (5.0) · actionability 5/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-09-10

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, legal, operations

Four coordinated actions under Operation Economic Outcast. On 8 September FinCEN issued an alert on procurement networks supporting Iran's commercial aviation sector, addressed to depository institutions, MSBs, casinos, insurers, securities and futures firms, mortgage firms and precious metals dealers, while OFAC sanctioned 36 linked entities and individuals. On 10 September OFAC designated a further 16 individuals and 5 entities across Iraq, Lebanon, Syria and the UAE; moved its Iran licensing policy to a presumption of denial except as required by law or in narrow circumstances such as risk to life, limb or environmental safety; and announced a $1,427,230 settlement with an individual, graded egregious and not voluntarily self-disclosed, for conduct including management consulting to an Iranian software company, receiving Iranian-origin dividends into US accounts and acquiring Iranian real property. FinCEN issued a whistleblower bulletin the same day. Read together these are a campaign design: the licensing route that bought time is shut, personal liability has a price on ordinary professional conduct, and the person most likely to report a firm's Iran exposure now has a financial incentive and a named channel. Continues the thread at 2026-08-25-us-002.

**Action:** Re-assess any strategy that depended on a pending or planned Iran licence application. Review private banking and wealth books for clients holding Iranian-origin assets or income, since the settlement conduct is that profile rather than a sanctions-evasion network. Apply the aviation procurement red flags in trade finance and aviation lending. Confirm internal speak-up routes are credible enough that staff use them before the external one.

Source: <https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260910> · re-checked 2026-09-14

## OFAC designates Xinbi Guarantee, the escrow marketplace behind Southeast Asian scam centres, with its messaging app and crypto wallet developers

**OFAC · US · sanctions action**

Materiality: **High** (4.6) · actionability 5/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-09

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, e money institutions, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, technology, data, operations

Xinbi Guarantee, SafeW Technology Co., Ltd. (Singapore messaging app developer) and Anwen Technology Co., Ltd. (Cambodia crypto wallet developer) designated under EO 13581. Treasury says Xinbi has processed over $24bn in digital assets and fiat since around 2022, acting as escrow and marketplace for the goods and services scam operations run on. The UK FCDO sanctioned Xinbi on 26 March 2026 and the DOJ Scam Center Strike Force seized associated infrastructure and wallets. Los Tiguerones was designated the same day and ten FAQs amended, two issued. The target is infrastructure rather than perpetrators. The weakness is transmission: an SDN entry is a set of names, while the exposure is blockchain addresses and app publisher identities. In most firms sanctions screening and crypto analytics are separate tools, owned by separate teams, and never reconciled, so the designation only bites where something joins them.

**Action:** Test whether a designated entity name can be turned into the addresses, clusters or app publisher identities the firm would actually block, and who owns that step. Check whether the blockchain analytics vendor attributes these entities and whether that attribution feeds screening. Review Southeast Asia correspondent and payment exposure and investment-scam victim reports for the corridor.

Source: <https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0624> · re-checked 2026-09-14

## FinCEN and the federal banking agencies classify mobile driver's licences as documentary ID under CIP, and put third-party digital credentials on the non-documentary side

**FinCEN · US · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-08

*Who it catches:* credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* onboarding, financial crime, compliance, technology

FAQs issued jointly with staff of the FRB, FDIC, NCUA and OCC - two new, one updated - on state-issued mobile driver's licences and other government-issued verifiable digital credentials under the Customer Identification Program rule. A bank or credit union may accept them where it can extract and process the information and its CIP policy allows. The permission is the headline; the classification is the substance. A government-issued verifiable digital credential is a documentary method. An electronic credential obtained from a third party is non-documentary, and a bank using that route must satisfy itself the third party applies the authentication the bank itself would - which is a vendor assurance programme, not a policy amendment. The fraud surface moves too: nobody forges a signed credential, they obtain a genuine one in the wrong name or take over the wallet holding it, so issuance and account recovery become the control, and issuance sits with a state motor vehicle agency. Permissive rather than mandatory, hence actionability 3.

**Action:** Before permitting digital credentials in CIP policy, map which credentials will be accepted and by which route. For any third-party route, scope the authentication assurance the FAQs require of the provider. Ask the fraud function whether device binding and account takeover signals have been re-weighted for credentials that cannot be forged but can be misissued.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-issues-frequently-asked-questions-regarding-treatment-verifiable-digital> · re-checked 2026-09-14

## FTC proposed order requires payment processor Humboldt Merchant Services to pay $12m for processing shell merchants and moving them onto lower-risk BINs to evade fraud monitoring

**FTC · US · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-09-08

*Who it catches:* payment service providers, credit institutions  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, onboarding, risk

Proposed order filed in the Eastern District of Michigan on a 2-0 Commission vote, subject to court approval. The FTC alleges Humboldt processed for more than 1,000 shell merchant accounts fronting scam operations despite chargeback rates roughly ten times industry norms, and deliberately placed risky accounts on lower-risk bank identification numbers to evade card-network fraud monitoring. The order requires $12m in consumer redress and permanently bans credit card laundering, load balancing to evade monitoring, and processing for four merchant categories including straw companies and MATCH-listed merchants. The distinction that matters is monitoring evasion rather than monitoring failure - a control that worked and was routed around - and that is what draws permanent conduct bans rather than remediation. For sponsor banks and firms upstream, "our processor is regulated" describes a status, not a control. Scored consistently with the FTC Doxo record at 2026-08-23-us-001.

**Action:** Request chargeback distribution by BIN from processors in the chain, and establish whether anyone internally can produce or interpret it. Check whether merchant underwriting for sponsored relationships is assured independently or relied on as reported by the processor.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/ftc-takes-action-against-payment-processor-humboldt-merchant-services-knowingly-facilitating-payment> · re-checked 2026-09-14

## AMLA consults on a common EU format for suspicious transaction reports and transaction-record requests, closing 20 September

**AMLA · EU · consultation paper** · Article 69(3), Regulation (EU) 2024/1624

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-07-02 · consultation close 2026-09-20

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, technology, data

Draft Implementing Technical Standards under Article 69(3) AMLR setting a common format for reporting suspicions - with templates adapted to different types of obliged entity - and separate templates for credit and financial institutions providing transaction records to FIUs. Opened 2 July; public hearing held 9 September; closes 20 September 2026 at 23:59 CEST. Recorded this week because the hearing fell in the window, although publication pre-dates it. A common format makes reporting quality comparable across 27 FIUs for the first time, removing the cover that narrative quality has given weak reports. The transaction-record template is the sharper end: it standardises what an FIU can demand and how fast, which turns a question of willingness into one of data architecture. Firms that file by system integration will implement the template directly, so the mapping work lands in engineering rather than compliance.

**Action:** Respond by 20 September 2026, 23:59 CEST, if at all. Independently of the response, test whether transaction records could be produced from core systems in the draft template's structure, and who would do the mapping.

Source: <https://www.amla.europa.eu/policy/public-consultations/consultation-draft-its-format-reporting-suspicions-and-providing-transaction-records_en> · re-checked 2026-09-14

## UK statutory guidance on the Iran sanctions regime rewritten for the Iran (Sanctions) (Amendments) Regulations 2026, a day before OFAC tightened Iran licensing

**FCDO · UK · guidelines** · Iran (Sanctions) (Amendments) Regulations 2026

Materiality: **Medium** (3.0) · actionability 3/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-09-09

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, legal

Updated statutory guidance covering designated persons and the asset freeze, internal repression goods, interception and monitoring technology, and strategically important goods, with the financial services and brokering prohibitions attached to each. OFSI amended its supplementary licensing guidance on permitted travel expenses the next day. Statutory guidance is what a court and a supervisor read when judging whether controls were reasonable, so an amendment to reflect a new statutory instrument moves the baseline - and a procedure that links to the guidance rather than restating it has changed underneath its owners without notice. The timing matters for firms on both sides of the Atlantic: the UK refreshed its Iran framework on 9 September and OFAC moved to a presumption of denial on 10 September (2026-09-14-us-002). A compliance model built on assumed UK-US divergence misprices a week in which, on Iran, they converged. The OFSI organisation page also lists "Iran nuclear sanctions: guidance" under 9 September, but that publication page shows a last update of 1 May 2026; not relied on.

**Action:** Re-read the Iran procedures that cite this guidance by link and confirm they still reflect it. For firms with UK and US nexus, check whether Iran controls assume divergent regimes and whether that assumption still holds. Review live or planned OFSI licence applications against the amended supplementary guidance.

Source: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/iran-sanctions-guidance> · re-checked 2026-09-14
