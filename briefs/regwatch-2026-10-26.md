## OCC final rule for permitted payment stablecoin issuers and custody activities takes effect 18 January 2027, with the Bank Secrecy Act, AML and OFAC provisions left to a later rulemaking

**OCC · US · rule final** · NR 2026-92; GENIUS Act Regulations Final Rule; OCC Bulletins 2026-50, 2026-51, 2026-52

Materiality: **High** (4.0) · actionability 4/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-10-21 · effective 2027-01-18 · application date 2027-01-18

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, risk, legal, finance, board

The OCC's final rule sets requirements for permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers under OCC jurisdiction, and for certain custody activities by OCC-supervised entities. The rule and the GENIUS Act take effect on 18 January 2027. The ordering is the point: the OCC states that the rule addresses its required GENIUS Act regulations "except those on the Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money laundering, and OFAC sanctions," which "will be addressed in a separate rulemaking" in coordination with the Department of the Treasury, with no timeline given. So a licensing regime commences with its financial crime chapter absent, and an issuer authorised under it has a prudential rulebook and no rule-level BSA, AML or sanctions expectations written for its activity. That does not suspend the underlying statutory obligations, which is the trap: firms reading the final rule as the perimeter of their compliance duty will build to a rulebook that is missing its financial crime section rather than to the Bank Secrecy Act itself. Separately, the OCC says it will announce in a later news release when it begins accepting applications and waiver requests, so no application date exists yet and none is recorded. Scored actionability 4 rather than 5: binding with a dated commencement, but roughly a three-month window and contained to one activity. Reach 4 as a defined licence category. `deadline_type` is `application_date` as the day the rules begin to apply.

**Action:** Prospective and current payment stablecoin issuers and OCC-supervised custodians should build to the Bank Secrecy Act and OFAC obligations directly rather than waiting for the second rulemaking, and should record that the final rule is not the perimeter of their financial crime duty. Watch for the separate OCC news release opening applications and waiver requests, since no date for it is published.

Source: <https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-92.html> · re-checked 2026-11-07

## FCA consults on removing the Certification Regime and retiring the Directory of certified and assessed persons, responses due 22 January 2027

**FCA · UK · consultation paper** · CP26/37, Senior Managers and Certification Regime Review, Phase 2

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-10-22 · consultation close 2027-01-22

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, risk, board

The FCA proposes removing certain Senior Management Functions and making other roles notified rather than approved, streamlining the SMF application process, raising thresholds so fewer firms fall in the Enhanced regime, ending ongoing submission of Statements of Responsibilities and Management Responsibilities Maps, streamlining Prescribed Responsibilities, removing the Certification Regime, retiring the Directory of certified and assessed persons, and simplifying Conduct Rule training while raising breach reporting thresholds. Scope is all firms already subject to the SM&CR including third-country branches, and firms due to come into scope such as cryptoasset firms. Responses close 22 January 2027. The second-order consequence is that certification was never the control; it was the artefact evidencing that somebody had performed one. Removing it leaves the fitness and propriety obligation where it was, with no external record to point at, which lands very differently at a firm that ran certification as a real competence assessment than at one that ran it as an annual attestation sweep. The retirement of the Directory is the under-priced part, because that record is currently used by other firms for reference checking and for due diligence on intermediaries, so the information moves inside the firm holding it and becomes harder to obtain for everyone else. Scored actionability 2: direction of travel, with planning assumptions moving rather than any present obligation. Reach 5 as the whole of a major sector in a major jurisdiction. **Two figures from the 26 October edition's drafts are not recorded** because neither could be confirmed on this page today: a population of about 139,000 certified people, for which the FCA page gives no number, and those people coming off the Financial Services Register, where the FCA's own wording is that the Directory of certified and assessed persons will be retired. The Directory sits within the Register, so the edition's phrasing is loose rather than wrong, but the FCA's term is the one to use. Continues the thread at 2026-10-26-uk-002.

**Action:** Decide whether to respond by 22 January 2027. Independently of responding, establish whether the firm's certification process produces a competence assessment that would survive the removal of the certificate, since that assessment is what the retained fitness and propriety duty will rest on. Firms that rely on the Directory for reference checking or intermediary due diligence should identify what replaces it.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-37-senior-managers-certification-regime-review-phase-2> · re-checked 2026-11-07

## PRA consults on removing pre-approval for almost half of Senior Manager Functions and ending certification for about 7,700 individuals, responses due 22 January 2027

**PRA · UK · consultation paper** · CP14/26, Review of the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, Phase 2

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-10-22 · consultation close 2027-01-22

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, insurers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, risk, board

Issued alongside the FCA's CP26/37. The PRA proposes replacing pre-approval with notification for almost half of Senior Manager Functions, naming Chief Actuaries and Chairs of Risk and Audit Committees as examples, while keeping pre-approval for roles such as Chief Executive, Chief Finance and Chief Risk. Firms would remain responsible for assessing candidates' fitness and propriety. Fitness and propriety assessments, certificates and regulatory references would no longer be required for about 7,700 certified individuals, replaced by an ongoing duty to check competence and personal characteristics. Other proposals include fewer Statements of Responsibilities and Management Responsibilities Maps submissions, removal of several forms including Forms J, M and F, a temporary SMF cover period extended from 12 to 18 weeks, time-limited and conditional approvals, and a higher reporting threshold for some conduct rule breaches. The PRA estimates roughly 1,200 firms in scope, including about 675 deposit takers and 560 insurers. Responses close 22 January 2027. Implementation depends on the Financial Services and Markets Bill and is expected in H2 2027, which is the detail most worth carrying forward, because it means the planning horizon is a legislative dependency rather than a regulatory timetable and can move. Scored actionability 2 as direction of travel. Reach 4 as a defined set of licence categories, roughly 1,200 PRA-authorised firms, which is narrower than the FCA's population and is why the two records band differently. Continues the thread at 2026-10-26-uk-001. The 7,700 figure is PRA-only and is not a proxy for the FCA-supervised population.

**Action:** Dual-regulated firms should read this alongside CP26/37 and respond once, by 22 January 2027, to both where the proposals interact. Note the H2 2027 implementation expectation and its dependence on the Financial Services and Markets Bill when setting any internal project date, rather than planning to the consultation close.

Source: <https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2026/october/review-of-the-smcr-phase-two-consultation-paper> · re-checked 2026-11-07
