## On 10 December 2026, no transmittals from or to MEXC Global

**FinCEN · US · rule final** · Imposition of Special Measure Prohibiting the Transmittal of Funds Involving MEXC Global; Federal Register document 2026-22962, 91 FR 72420, published 10 November 2026; order under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act, as amended

Materiality: **High** (5.0) · actionability 5/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-11-06 · effective 2026-12-10 · compliance date 2026-12-10

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, money service businesses, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, operations, technology

FinCEN announced on 6 November, under Operation Economic Outcast, an order that it says prevents US financial institutions from processing funds transfers involving MEXC Global, a convertible virtual currency exchanger FinCEN says facilitated at least 16 billion dollars in illicit transactions linked to Iranian, North Korean and Russian sanctions evasion and to cybercrime. The order itself, read on 2026-11-10, is issued under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act (Pub. L. 116-283), as amended by section 6106(b) of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022 (Pub. L. 117-81), amending 31 CFR Chapter X, Part 1010. It finds MEXC Global, including its subsidiaries, branches, offices and successor entities operating outside the United States, to be a financial institution of primary money laundering concern in connection with Russian illicit finance. Its operative test is that a covered financial institution is prohibited from engaging in any transmittal of funds from or to MEXC Global, where a covered financial institution has the meaning of financial institution in 31 CFR 1010.100(t) and a transmittal of funds is the sending and receiving of funds, including CVC. "From or to" is narrower than the release's "involving" wording this record was first written from. The order is effective 30 days after Federal Register publication on 10 November 2026, so 10 December 2026. The second-order consequence is a control-design one rather than a screening one: an order against a named institution does not arrive through a sanctions data feed, so the obligation has to be converted by hand into detection logic for the counterparty and its aliases and addresses, with an evidenced go-live date before 10 December. Actionability 5: a binding obligation with a dated effective date that requires a systems change. Reach 5 because the prohibition binds US financial institutions generally, and non-US firms inherit it through dollar correspondents. instrument_type is rule_final because taxonomy.md has no value for a special measure order against a named institution; it is consistent with the proposed special measure at 2026-10-06-us-001, recorded as rule_proposed.

**Action:** Before 10 December 2026, convert the order into a production payment-blocking rule for MEXC Global and its known aliases and addresses, record the date it went live, and run a retrospective search of payment and counterparty data for prior exposure. The test to build to is the order's: a covered financial institution, meaning a financial institution as defined in 31 CFR 1010.100(t), is prohibited from engaging in any transmittal of funds from or to MEXC Global, including transfers of CVC.

## On 5 November 2026, OFAC designated 45 Prince Group-linked targets and one cyber target, reaching the corporate infrastructure around scam compounds

**OFAC · US · sanctions action** · Designations under Executive Order 13581 as amended and Executive Order 13694 as further amended; implementation of Executive Order 14390

Materiality: **High** (5.0) · actionability 5/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-11-05 · effective 2026-11-05

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, onboarding, operations, data

OFAC designated 45 targets under Executive Order 13581 as amended and one under Executive Order 13694 as further amended. Forty-five relate to the Prince Group transnational criminal organization: four individuals and forty-one entities spanning construction, logistics, hotels, insurance, property development, gambling, airlines and technology services in Cambodia, Hong Kong, the British Virgin Islands, Singapore, Mauritius and Malaysia. The separate cyber designation is a Malaysian national based in Madagascar whom Treasury says led a scam network. The action builds on designations of 14 October 2025 and 23 June 2026. The second-order consequence is that this is a beneficial ownership exercise in the shape of a sanctions action: with 50 percent rule aggregation across multi-jurisdiction holding structures, the blocked population is larger than the published list, and the control under test is whether screening reaches through the corporate layer. Blocked property must be reported to OFAC and civil penalties may apply on a strict liability basis. Scored 5 and 5: binding and immediate, reaching all US persons and, through dollar exposure, well beyond them. Continues the thread at 2026-09-07-us-001 and 2026-09-14-us-003, the FinCEN scam-centre alert and the Xinbi Guarantee designation.

**Action:** Ingest the SDN additions, then re-run ownership aggregation across the counterparty book rather than relying on name matching alone, and evidence that re-run. Report blocked property to OFAC and review exposure in sectors not normally treated as sanctions-sensitive, including hospitality, insurance and logistics.

## On 5 November 2026, the EBA published revised internal governance Guidelines under CRD VI, with a lighter duties mapping and a framework for third-country branches

**EBA · EU · guidelines** · EBA/GL/2026/10, Guidelines on internal governance under Directive 2013/36/EU, issued under Article 74 of that Directive

Materiality: **High** (4.4) · actionability 4/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-11-05

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* board, compliance, risk, legal

The revised Guidelines reflect CRD VI, which adds a mapping of duties and responsibilities for management body members and key function holders and a governance framework for third-country branches. Against the consultation draft, the final version reduces the detail required for the duties mapping and individual statements, applies more proportionality to third-country branches, simplifies conflict-of-interest provisions including where the Chair and an executive role are combined, and reduces the granularity of diversity and inclusion monitoring. The second-order consequence is that each reduction in a required artefact moves the burden of proof from the document to the behaviour: the duties mapping exists to turn a collective board responsibility into a named one, and reducing its granularity does not reduce the responsibility it records. The third-country branch framework is the quieter change, giving a measurable standard to branches that previously ran on group governance by assertion. The final report, read on 2026-11-10, states that the Guidelines apply from two months after publication in all EU official languages and that EBA/GL/2021/05 is repealed from a date not yet filled in; the EBA lists them as final and awaiting translation, so no application date exists yet and effective is left null rather than estimated. Scored actionability 4 as final text with a defined application period where only publication of the translations is outstanding. Reach 5 as it covers the EU banking sector.

**Action:** Watch for publication of the translations, which starts the two-month period, and record the resulting application date. Re-test the duties mapping and key function holder statements against the reduced granularity, and confirm that the allocation of duties can be evidenced from how the organisation actually operates, not only from the mapping document. Third-country branches should gap-assess against the new governance framework.

## From 24 June 2027, ESG rating providers may apply for FCA authorisation, with the UK regime taking effect 29 June 2028

**FCA · UK · rule final** · PS26/22, ESG ratings, final regime

Materiality: **Medium** (3.6) · actionability 4/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-11-03 · effective 2028-06-29 · submission due 2027-06-24

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, board, legal, risk, data

The FCA published its final regime bringing ESG rating providers into UK regulation for the first time, covering transparency, governance, systems and controls, management of conflicts of interest, and engagement with users of ratings and with rated entities. It regulates how ratings are produced, governed and communicated and does not prescribe a methodology. It follows CP25/34, open 1 December 2025 to 31 March 2026, and the October 2025 legislation that brought ESG ratings within the FCA's remit. Providers must begin applying from 24 June 2027 in staggered periods, and from 29 June 2028 providing ESG ratings in the UK requires authorisation and the relevant permission unless an exemption applies; the FCA has published scoping guidance. The second-order consequence sits with who has to build a compliance function from nothing: many providers are data, research, index and analytics businesses with no conflicts register, no systems and controls documentation and no authorisation experience, and the binding constraint is the governance evidence that only accrues through a year of operation. For consumers of ratings the effect lands earlier, in 2027 vendor review, if a provider does not intend to apply. Scored actionability 4 as binding with a long implementation window, reach 3 as a defined business model rather than a licensed sector. deadline_type is submission_due, the taxonomy value for an authorisation gateway application, as at 2026-10-06-uk-001; note that 24 June 2027 is the date the application window opens, not a filing deadline, because the release does not give the closing dates of the staggered periods. entity_scope is recorded as all_regulated_firms because the taxonomy has no value for a newly regulated non-financial provider; the population is narrower than that label.

**Action:** Providers: reach and document a scoping conclusion against the FCA's guidance, naming the decision maker and recording exclusions, then begin generating the governance and conflicts evidence an application will require, and plan for a staggered slot that may fall well before June 2028. Users of ESG ratings: add authorisation intent to 2027 vendor due diligence.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/new-fca-rules-bring-esg-rating-providers-uk-regulation-first-time> · re-checked 2026-11-09

## On 3 November 2026, the FCA set conditions on motor finance settlement offers made while the redress scheme is partially suspended

**FCA · UK · supervisory statement** · FCA expectations for firms making car finance settlement offers

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-11-03

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, operations, risk, legal

With the motor finance redress scheme partially suspended while legal challenges are heard between 16 February and 3 March 2027, the FCA set out expectations for lenders choosing to make settlement offers: customers must be offered no less than they would receive through the scheme; lenders must explain that accepting means keeping the money even if the legal outcome would have paid less and that no further claim can be made if it would have paid more; the FCA's factsheet must be shared; lenders must notify the FCA of an intention to make offers and the FCA will publish a list of those doing so; and offers cannot be referred to the Financial Ombudsman Service because a customer may reject an offer and leave the claim to the scheme. Barclays Partner Finance has confirmed it will make offers to some customers. Firms must continue preparing for the scheme and progressing complaints but need not calculate or pay compensation until the legal process concludes. The second-order consequence is that "no less than the scheme would pay" is a calculation obligation inside a settlement condition: a compliant offer requires a scheme-equivalent calculation, with versioned assumptions and a record of which version applied to which customer, before the methodology is finally settled. Scored actionability 3 as a supervisory expectation firms must evidence a position against, reach 4 as a defined sector. entity_scope is recorded as all_regulated_firms because the taxonomy has no value for motor finance lenders; the population is narrower than that label. board is not listed in functions_affected because the FCA does not require a governing-body decision; taking the decision at board level is this register's recommendation, not an obligation.

**Action:** Lenders intending to make offers: notify the FCA, share the factsheet, and put in place a versioned scheme-equivalence calculation with a per-customer record of the assumptions applied, so consistency across the book can be demonstrated once the methodology is settled. Take the decision to offer at board level given the published list.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-sets-expectations-firms-making-car-finance-settlement-offers> · re-checked 2026-11-09

## On 6 November 2026, the EBA deferred the first supervisory reporting reference date to December 2027, with liquidity, encumbrance, benchmarking and ESG to December 2028

**EBA · EU · technical standard** · Draft ITS revising Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3117 on supervisory reporting

Materiality: **Medium** (3.2) · actionability 2/5 · reach 5/5

Published 2026-11-06

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* finance, risk, data, technology, compliance

Following consultation feedback the EBA set out how it will finalise its supervisory reporting simplification, adding further simplification, more proportionality and longer implementation timelines. Changes include reduced collateral, stress-testing and ESG reporting, integration of credit risk and IFRS 9 benchmarking into supervisory reporting, simplified operational risk loss reporting, and a dedicated stress-testing reporting module. The first reference date for new requirements moves to December 2027, with liquidity, asset encumbrance, supervisory benchmarking and ESG reporting deferred to December 2028. The final report is due in December 2026 and the technical package, covering the data point model, taxonomies and validation rules, in early 2027, with institutions given at least a one-year implementation period. Scored 2 on actionability because the ITS is draft and not in force, which the rubric treats as a 2 regardless of planning significance; reach 5 as it covers all institutions subject to EU supervisory reporting. The effective and deadline fields are null because the stated dates are month-level only and cannot be expressed as a date; they are recorded in this rationale instead.

**Action:** Move regulatory reporting change plans onto the December 2027 and December 2028 reference dates, and schedule a read of the December 2026 final report and the early-2027 technical package before committing build work. Institutions in the benchmarking exercise should keep applying the 2026 benchmarking ITS during the transition.

## On 2 November 2026, the FTC and Washington State settled with a payment processor over hidden fees, auto-renewal and cancellation friction

**FTC · US · enforcement** · FTC and State of Washington v Riverside Payments Inc, Cascade Equipment Leasing LLC and two individuals, W.D. Wash.

Materiality: **Medium** (3.0) · actionability 3/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-11-02

*Who it catches:* payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, operations

The FTC and the State of Washington brought an action against a payment processor, an affiliated equipment leasing company and two owners over savings claims of 40 to 60 percent that were not borne out, omitted activation, batch, data-security non-compliance and inactive-processing fees, a 26-page electronic contract that did not clearly disclose automatic renewal or a 695 dollar early termination fee, promised no-risk cancellation that was difficult to exercise, and sales agent earnings claims of 75,000 to 95,000 dollars against an actual average of 3,787 dollars. Counts were brought under the FTC Act and the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, the latter for obtaining consumers' financial information through false or fraudulent statements. Defendants pay 600,000 dollars with a 32,083,921 dollar judgment suspended on inability to pay, and must notify customers of a 60-day waiver of the early termination fee. Scored actionability 3 under the enforcement guidance in rubric.md: the reasoning generalises, because the Gramm-Leach-Bliley pretexting count and the negative-option disclosure requirements apply to any business that collects billing information behind an auto-renewing contract, so firms outside the matter should be able to evidence a position on them. Consistent with the FTC payment processor enforcement records at 2026-09-07-us-003 and 2026-09-14-us-004, both scored 3. Reach 3 as a sub-population defined by business model: merchant services and other sellers on auto-renewing contracts.

**Action:** Where the firm sells on auto-renewing contracts, test that all material terms, renewal and termination fees are disclosed before billing information is taken, that consent to negative-option billing is express, and that a simple cancellation route exists and works when tested end to end.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/ftc-washington-take-action-against-payment-processor-deceiving-small-businesses-about-costs> · re-checked 2026-11-09

## FinCEN and the Federal Reserve withdrew the 2020 proposal to cut the cross-border funds transfer recordkeeping and travel rule threshold from 3,000 to 250 dollars

**FinCEN · US · rule withdrawn** · Federal Register document 2026-22809, 91 FR 70824, published 9 November 2026; withdrawal of the NPRM at 85 FR 68005 (27 October 2020), RIN 1506-AB41

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-11-06

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, money service businesses, payment service providers, crypto asset service providers  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, data, technology

FinCEN announced on 6 November the withdrawal, jointly with the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, of a notice of proposed rulemaking that would have amended recordkeeping and travel rule requirements for convertible virtual currencies and lowered the threshold for funds transfers and transmittals of funds that begin or end outside the United States. The Federal Register notice, read on 2026-11-10, identifies it as the joint NPRM of 27 October 2020, which would have reduced the collect-and-retain threshold, and FinCEN's transmit threshold, from 3,000 to 250 dollars for cross-border transfers, and would have clarified that "money" in the rules covers convertible virtual currency and digital assets with legal tender status. The withdrawal took effect as of 9 November 2026 and the agencies state they will take no further action on the NPRM. Neither the release nor the notice restates what remains in force. The existing recordkeeping and travel rule obligations are untouched: this removes a proposal, not a duty. Scored 2 on actionability because a proposal that is not in force is direction of travel under the rubric whatever its news value; the consequence is that planning assumptions move, since programmes built around a lower cross-border threshold lose their driver and deferred data remediation loses its justification. Reach 4 covers banks, money transmitters and virtual asset service providers with cross-border flows rather than the whole sector. instrument_type follows taxonomy.md and the 6 October withdrawals. Continues the thread at 2026-10-12-us-002.

**Action:** Re-baseline any 2027 change programme that assumed a lower cross-border recordkeeping threshold, and re-justify or restart data remediation that was deferred pending the proposal. Do not treat the withdrawal as relaxing existing recordkeeping or travel rule obligations.

Source: <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-announces-withdrawal-proposed-rule-related-recordkeeping-and-travel-rule> · re-checked 2026-11-10

## On 6 November 2026, the Commission announced an EU Crypto Sanctions Task Force to detect circumvention of financial sanctions through crypto assets

**EC · EU · supervisory statement** · Statement by Commissioner Albuquerque on the tenth meeting of the High-Level Expert Group on Union Restrictive Measures

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-11-06

*Who it catches:* crypto asset service providers, credit institutions, payment service providers  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, risk

The statement reports the tenth meeting of the High-Level Expert Group on Union Restrictive Measures, held 5 November with Member State representatives, the Commission and the European External Action Service, covering emerging financial threats including crypto assets, the role of the European Anti-Fraud Office in investigating sanctions circumvention, and Member States' efforts to enforce measures consistently. It announces the launch of an EU Crypto Sanctions Task Force. It sets no obligations, deadlines or guidance and does not describe the task force's mandate, membership or timeline. The second-order consequence is directional: pairing OLAF with crypto circumvention signals investigative capability being built before any rule, so questions about crypto exposure will begin arriving from a part of the system that does not supervise the firm and the answers will have to be assembled rather than retrieved. The acknowledgement of inconsistent national enforcement matters to any group operating across several Member States, because the expectation of uniform application is being raised ahead of the capability to enforce it uniformly. Scored 2 on actionability as direction of travel with no action required, reach 4 as it bears on a defined set of licence categories.

**Action:** Record the task force as a planning assumption for 2027 sanctions work, and establish now whether crypto-asset exposure can be evidenced on demand for sanctions purposes, including counterparties currently treated as out of scope because they are not payment institutions.

## By 31 December 2026, firms must register interest to join the FCA's Quantum Experimentation Group on money laundering and fraud detection

**FCA · UK · report** · FCA Quantum Experimentation Group

Materiality: **Low** (2.0) · actionability 2/5 · reach 2/5

Published 2026-11-05 · consultation close 2026-12-31

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, technology, data

The FCA announced the Quantum Experimentation Group, which it describes as the first regulator-led group of its kind, to test initially whether quantum machine learning can help detect money laundering and fraud. The FCA will provide a synthetic dataset to address the difficulty firms have in obtaining suitable test data, and participants may include financial services firms, quantum technology providers and national research laboratories at varying stages of maturity. Interest must be registered by 31 December 2026 and the group launches in Q1 2027. The release sets no new obligations. Scored 2 and 2: voluntary participation in a narrow activity. The record exists because the 31 December date is real and belongs on the calendar, and because the synthetic dataset, if its labelling is published, is the more reusable output than any quantum result; the register's convention is that the band governs treatment, not whether an item is kept. deadline_type is consultation_close, following 2026-11-02-eu-001 and 2026-10-06-eu-003 for a voluntary call for expressions of interest: submission_due is reserved for one-off filings whose miss costs the right to carry on an activity.

**Action:** Firms wanting to participate must register interest with the FCA by 31 December 2026. Others should note Q1 2027 for any published labelling methodology behind the synthetic dataset, which is reusable for conventional detection model evaluation.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-explore-quantum-computing-tackle-financial-crime> · re-checked 2026-11-09
