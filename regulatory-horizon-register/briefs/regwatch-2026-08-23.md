## FCA warns on unregulated mini-bonds and loan notes, and states an expectation that banks, PSPs and professional firms report suspicious distribution

**FCA · UK · supervisory statement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-20

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, payment service providers, designated non financial businesses  
*Whose desk:* financial crime, compliance, onboarding

A consumer warning on unregulated loan notes and mini-bonds, using the failure of litigation funder Woodville Consultants Ltd as the case study; the FCA states it has issued over 1,200 warnings in 2026. Marketing speculative illiquid securities to retail investors has been banned since 1 January 2021. The industry-facing part is the significant one: the FCA cannot supervise an issuer outside the perimeter, so it supervises the firms that touch the money instead, and names banks, payment providers, lawyers, accountants and auditors as expected to report suspicious distribution. Affected customers have no FOS or FSCS recourse, which concentrates recovery pressure on whoever remains solvent and regulated.

**Action:** Confirm whether the FCA warning list is an input to adverse-media and customer screening. Be able to evidence what the firm did on becoming aware of an in-scope issuer among its customers or counterparties.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/consumers-warned-beware-risky-mini-bonds-and-loan-notes> · re-checked 2026-08-24

## Doxo and two co-founders to pay $2.1m to settle FTC allegations of biller-impersonating search ads, undisclosed fees and non-consented subscription enrolment

**FTC · US · enforcement**

Materiality: **Medium** (3.4) · actionability 3/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-17

*Who it catches:* payment service providers, money service businesses  
*Whose desk:* compliance, financial crime, operations

Following a 2024 complaint and partial summary judgment, the FTC announced a proposed settlement under which Doxo and co-founders Steve Shivers and Roger Parks pay $2.1m in consumer redress. The allegation was that search ads and landing pages carrying billers' names and logos presented Doxo as an official payment channel where no relationship existed, with undisclosed delivery fees and subscription enrolment; a court found a Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act violation. The order is proposed and filed in federal court in Washington state, not yet finally entered. What makes this a control question rather than a news item is that the respondent is a funded, incorporated company with a compliance function, not an external fraudster — which is precisely what impersonation detection is not built to catch.

**Action:** Establish whether the firm's paid-search keyword set and ad copy have ever been reviewed by second line, and who owns that review. Confirm whether brand-term bidding by third parties is monitored by the fraud function.

Source: <https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/bill-payment-firm-doxo-pay-21-million-settle-ftc-allegations-it-deceived-consumers-charged-them-add> · re-checked 2026-08-24

## FCA fines and bans former SVS Securities CEO Demetrios Hadjigeorgiou; penalty reduced on reclassification of the conduct

**FCA · UK · enforcement** · Final Notice 2026 — Demetrios Christos Hadjigeorgiou

Materiality: **Medium** (2.8) · actionability 2/5 · reach 4/5

Published 2026-08-19

*Who it catches:* all regulated firms, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, legal, board

Prohibition from senior management functions plus a £56,400 penalty. While CEO from May 2018 to August 2019, SVS invested customer money including pension savings in high-risk products issued by parties making significant payments to the firm, and he failed to challenge a 10% markdown applied to customers' bond investments on sale, generating £359,800 for SVS. The operative detail is not the fine: an April 2024 Decision Notice proposed £84,600, and on settlement — with the referral to the Upper Tribunal withdrawn — the FCA left its factual findings unchanged but reclassified the markdown conduct from a want of integrity to a failure to exercise due skill, care and diligence under Statement of Principle 6, reducing the penalty. The same facts, differently characterised, produce a materially different sanction. That characterisation boundary is where SMCR defence arguments actually live.

**Action:** Monitor only. No obligation change. Worth reading the conduct sections of the Final Notice against current senior-manager fitness and propriety files, and noting the April 2024 to August 2026 interval when modelling enforcement timelines.

Source: <https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-bans-former-svs-securities-ceo> · re-checked 2026-08-24

## ESMA consults on the reporting framework for clearing activity at recognised third-country CCPs

**ESMA · EU · consultation paper**

Materiality: **Low** (2.4) · actionability 2/5 · reach 3/5

Published 2026-08-18 · consultation close 2026-10-12

*Who it catches:* credit institutions, investment firms  
*Whose desk:* compliance, operations, data

A consultation on how clearing activity at recognised third-country CCPs is to be reported. Low current materiality and high forward significance — the two are not the same thing. Reporting frameworks are how a supervisor assembles an evidence base before acting, and the question this one is built to answer is how much EU clearing still sits outside the EU. The obligation arriving now is data collection. Any relocation pressure would arrive later, on the strength of what the data shows.

**Action:** Consider responding. Feedback on the reporting framework, templates and format is invited by 12 October 2026, confirmed on the consultation page.
